# Overview
### Genre
- Political Satire
- Fable/Roman à clef
### Characters
#### Farmers
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Jones</mark>: the farmer who owned Manor farm, the man who exploited the animals. An allegory for the bourgeoisie who exploit the working class in a Capitalist system. 
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Pilkington</mark>: The owner of Foxwood. An easy-going farmer who spent the majority of his time hunting or fishing.
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Frederick</mark>: The owner of Pinchfield, a smaller farm which was maintained better.
#### Animals
- Old Major: an old and wise bore who tells the animals of revolution and rebellion, aswell as the lyrics to 'Beasts of England', sparking the uprising against Jones. An allegory for both Marx and Lenin, establishing Marxist-Leninism amongst the animals.

- Napoleon: A Berkshire bore, large, ruthless and loud. He serves as the protagonist, being the one who becomes the dictator of the farm. He serves as an allegory for Joseph Stalin and his loud, corrupt and bourgeoisie policy, favouring Authoritarianism and controlling the other animals. Not only that, but he raised nine dogs to serve as protectors to him, and to suppress any ideas of rebellion against his dictatorship. 

- Snowball: A younger pig who is a skilled orator, with excellent public speaking skills. He generally serves as the antagonist to Napoleon. He serves as an allegory for Leon Trotsky, generally favouring more equal laws, democratic processes through the Meetings and a more Libertarian approach. Furthermore, he taught the other animals how to read, promised to bring benefits to all the animals using the windmill, etc. Much like Trotsky, he was exiled by Napoleon.

- Boxer: A strong, very hardworking man. His primary maxim is to simply work harder when faced with a problem. Overall he isn't the smartest, being barely able to learn the first few letters of the alphabet, but he is very loyal to animalism. He is an allegory to Alexei Stakhanov, a Soviet coal miner who broke several records for his hard work. His maxims are used as a substitute for thinking, saying them instead of trying to reason or make an opinion.

- Clover: A kind, helpful motherly mare. She is similar to Boxer in both intelligence and loyalty to animalism. She has a great amount of empathy for the other animals and takes care of them. Much like Boxer, she struggled to understand complex topics, but still absorbed all they were taught by the pigs and spread it to the other animals.

- Mollie: A pretty, foolish and naïve white mare who enjoyed attention above all. A manifestation of prioritising aesthetics and attention over survival and morality. She betrayed the animals by meeting with Mr. Pilkington and eventually living with the humans. Her love for beauty and attention outshone her morals at every chance it received.

- The hens: Despite being largely powerless, they did try to protest when they could. Though their efforts art largely fruitless.

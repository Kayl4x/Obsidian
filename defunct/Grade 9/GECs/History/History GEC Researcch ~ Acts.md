# Bantu Education Act // n.47 - 1953

#### Causes
1) African education be funded by the general poll tax collected from Africans rather than from the General Revenue Account used to fund white education. [^^] (way worse funding, no tax burden on the White population.)[https://overcomingapartheid.msu.edu/sidebar/kid=163-581-2/]
2) The act required Black children to attend the government schools. [https://www.britannica.com/event/Bantu-Education-Act]
3) The National Party government saw the need for a trained African labour force. ["Bantu education and the racist compartmentalizing of education." South African History Online, 31 Mar. 2011, https://sahistory.org.za/ref/A-0059697. Accessed 19 Aug. 2026.]

#### Effects
- Government funding of black schools became conditional on acceptance of a racially discriminatory curriculum administered by a new Department of Bantu Education [https://overcomingapartheid.msu.edu/sidebar/kid=163-581-2/]
- Bantu education schools suffered terribly from government's neglect [^^]
- Even after the separate account was abolished in 1972, education of African children still remained grossly under-resourced, receiving one-tenth of the money afforded to whites and struggling with 56:1 student-teacher ratios (Hartshorne, 41).
- Dilapidated school buildings, overcrowded classrooms, inadequate instruction, poor teacher training, and a lack of textbooks plagued African education.
[https://overcomingapartheid.msu.edu/sidebar/kid=163-581-2/]

# Promotion of Bantu Self-Government // n.46 - 1959

### Causes
1) The chief aim behind this act was to eventually grant independence to the homelands (see the BANTU HOMELANDS CONSTITUTION ACT of 1971), and thereby depriving the Blacks of their South African citizenships (thus effectively increasing the percentage of Whites in South Africa).[https://omalley.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/site/q/03lv01538/04lv01828/05lv01829/06lv01899.htm]
2) Gave the government grounds to argue it was no longer politically responsible for Black South Africans, allowing it to abolish their already limited indirect parliamentary representation. ["The Promotion of Bantu Self-Government Act." Time Graphics, time.graphics/event/1952869.]

### Effects
1) By giving the homelands, and thus their indigenous people independence, they were stripped of South African citizenship, and thus, all parliamentary representation. [https://omalley.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/site/q/03lv01538/04lv01828/05lv01829/06lv01899.htm]
2) Academic research using long-run data (VoxDev, drawing on peer-reviewed economics work) found that being moved to the homelands during childhood significantly and precisely reduced educational attainment, with limited local job opportunities largely controlled by local government and requiring formal education few could access. [https://voxdev.org/topic/education/long-shadow-apartheid-how-forced-relocation-homelands-still-affects-south-africa]
3) In former resettlement areas today, people are 39% more likely to say others can generally be trusted, and report 30% less physical violence, than people in nearby areas that weren't used for resettlement. These differences persist across generations. [de Haas, Nonso. "Long-Run Effects of Forced Resettlement: Evidence from Apartheid South Africa." African Economic History Network, aehnetwork.org/blog/long-run-effects-of-forced-resettlement-evidence-from-apartheid-south-africa/]

Since childhood, I've always wanted to make things. I've always loved seeing the intricate little details of tech. and how everything works together. So I decided to aim for becoming a biomedical engineer.

I've always been a very curious and pragmatic person, so it's only natural that my inclinations are investigative and realistic. These fit perfectly with any line of engineering, as it naturally needs a lot of technical understanding and realism.

Furthermore, the biological aspect of biomedical engineering is also deeply investigative. It requires a lot of analysis, research and investigation, which is a perfect fit for me.

To actually be a biomedical engineer I would most likely need a bachelor's and honours degree of science, and finally a master of science in biomedical engineering.

Alternatively, I could get a bachelor's and honours degree of engineering, and then a master of engineering science in biomedical engineering.

Both paths should take "only" 10 years of my life, but I do certainly think it's worth it.

With a master's degree, I can look at about 600-700 thousand rand a year, and after just five years of experience I can earn about double that.

In the workplace, what a biomedical engineer actually does depends a lot on the field. For example, a genetic engineer and neurocomputing engineer would do very different things. Since I want to go more into neurocomputing, I would most likely do a lot of analysis and research.

In conclusion, biomedical engineering is the perfect career for me, as it combines everything I love and everything I'm good at.
